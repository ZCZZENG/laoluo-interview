# Variable Podcast Method

This reference adapts a "choice, cost, result" podcast workflow into reusable interview criteria.

## Channel Kernel

The program records how controllable variables shape long-term outcomes. It rejects success theater and focuses on choices that can be inspected.

Every episode should answer:

- Which variable changed?
- What real cost did the change create?
- Which part of the lesson transfers to another person?

## Topic Gate

For solo episodes, ask five yes/no questions:

1. Does this involve an adjustable variable?
2. Is there a real cost, failure, or tradeoff?
3. Can the story produce transferable experience?
4. Will the topic still matter one to three years later?
5. Will discussing it clarify the maker's own choices?

Require at least three "yes" answers. Otherwise reject or keep as private notes.

## Guest Preparation

Before formal recording, prepare at least one page of material:

- 50 to 80 word guest summary
- three experience points worth deep questioning
- public sources: posts, podcasts, videos, talks, profiles
- five to ten deep questions
- sensitive boundaries
- potential guest referrals

## Guest Red Flags

Stop before recording if any of these appear:

- strong demand to control title, conclusion, or framing
- repeated image-management pressure
- discomfort with "may not be published"
- inability to explain what variable actually changed
- only outcome narration, no process details

## Pre-Interview Positioning

Use a clear expectation-setting line:

> Before formal recording, I run a short pre-interview. It helps me decide whether the story fits the program and which variable is worth exploring. Not every pre-interview becomes a public episode.

This protects the producer's right to stop, delay, or redirect the episode.

## Pre-Interview Decision

After pre-interview, choose one:

- proceed to formal recording
- delay because the variable is immature
- stop because it does not fit the channel

Do not over-explain rejection. Use channel fit, not personal judgment.

## Three Things To Judge

### Variable awareness

The guest can say "I changed X" rather than "I worked hard" or "I was unlucky."

### Narrative clarity

The guest can describe the sequence concretely, even if not eloquently.

### Boundary respect

The guest accepts that recording is not guaranteed publication.

## Abort After Recording

Even after recording, ask:

1. Would I still recommend this episode six months later?
2. Would it attract the audience I clearly do not want?
3. Did it drift away from the variable-cost-result line?

If two are problematic, archive or delay instead of publishing.

## Solo Episode Rules

Record a solo episode when:

- a variable was changed
- a pitfall or stop-loss happened
- a structural observation emerged across multiple guests
- a future hypothesis needs to be declared

Do not record when it is only emotional release, pure summary, or ego defense.

For failure or conflict topics, require at least 14 days of cooling time or a second structural validation before publishing a conclusion.
